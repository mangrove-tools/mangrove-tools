How the budget model works

Budget Advisor combines marginality analysis with budget optimization to tell you where to put your next dollar.

  • Response curves — For each channel, a power-law curve is fitted to your historical spend and conversions. This captures diminishing returns: the first dollar in often has the highest return.
  • Marginal CPA — The cost of the next conversion at the current spend level. A low marginal CPA means a channel is efficient at the margin. A high marginal CPA means the next dollar would be better spent elsewhere.
  • Budget optimization — Given a total budget and per-channel min/max constraints, the model iteratively moves spend from low-marginal-ROI channels to high-marginal-ROI channels until equilibrium.

This is a simplified model — not a full marketing mix model. It works best when you have at least 3 months of historical spend and conversion data per channel, and at least 3-4 channels to compare.

Budget optimization FAQ

What data do I need?

You need historical spend and conversions (or revenue) for each marketing channel, broken down by time period. At least 3 months per channel is needed for the model to fit a reliable response curve.

What counts as a conversion?

A conversion is whatever matters to your business — a purchase, a lead form fill, a sign-up, a trial start. Enter the number that aligns with your business goal. The tool works the same way whether you track revenue or raw conversion volume.

How accurate are the recommendations?

The model uses statistical curve fitting, so accuracy depends on data quality and volume. With 6+ months of clean historical data across 4+ channels, results are directionally reliable. With sparse or noisy data, treat the output as a starting point rather than a precise answer.

Why does it use power-law curves?

Power-law (also called Cobb-Douglas in economics) is a standard approach for modeling diminishing returns in marketing spend. It captures the empirically observed pattern that doubling spend rarely doubles output — there is almost always a saturation point.

What if I only have 1-2 channels?

The model needs at least 2 channels with positive spend and conversions to fit response curves. If you only have one channel, there is no optimization to do — all budget goes there by definition.
