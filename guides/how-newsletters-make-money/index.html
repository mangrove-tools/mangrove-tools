How do newsletters make money?

Nearly every newsletter earns from some mix of four streams. Here is how each one works, when it fits, and how to put a number on it with a free calculator.

Estimate your revenue How the numbers work

“How much can a newsletter make?” is the wrong first question. The better one is: which streams fit my audience, and how big does the audience need to be for each? Almost all newsletter revenue comes from four sources. Most creators blend two or three.

1. Programmatic ads

Ad networks place ads in your emails and pay based on opens (impressions), usually quoted as an RPM — revenue per thousand opens. It is the easiest stream to switch on: connect a network, drop in an ad slot, get paid per send. The trade-off is rate. RPMs are modest and the network takes a share, so ads reward volume more than intimacy. Good for larger or broad-interest lists that want passive income without a sales process.

2. Direct sponsorships

Instead of a network, you sell a placement directly to a brand, priced with a CPM (cost per thousand opens) or a flat fee. Because there is no middleman, margins are high and rates in a valuable niche can dwarf programmatic. The cost is effort: you have to sell, negotiate, and deliver. Direct sponsorships can work surprisingly early — a few thousand engaged subscribers in finance, tech, or another high-intent niche can command real fees. Use SponsorQuote to set a defensible rate.

3. Paid subscriptions

You charge readers directly for premium content, usually a monthly or annual fee. This is the highest-intent stream: a small percentage of your list converts, but each one pays every month. Revenue is subscribers × conversion rate × price, so it scales with both audience size and how much value you paywall. If you have a revenue goal in mind, SubTarget works backward to the number of paying subscribers you need.

4. Affiliates

You recommend products you genuinely use and earn a commission when readers buy or sign up. Done honestly — after you’ve delivered value, with clear disclosure — affiliates convert well because they’re contextual. They pair naturally with any of the streams above and require no ad inventory. The risk is trust: promote junk and you burn the audience that makes the newsletter worth anything.

Which mix is right for you?

A rough rule of thumb:

  • Small, high-intent niche list: lead with direct sponsorships and affiliates; add a paid tier once you have a premium angle.
  • Large, broad list: programmatic ads become meaningful; layer sponsorships for premium sends.
  • Deep expertise your readers act on: paid subscriptions can become the core, with everything else secondary.
