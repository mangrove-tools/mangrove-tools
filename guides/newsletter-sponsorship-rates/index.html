How to set newsletter sponsorship rates

The number one question from creators with a sponsor knocking: “what do I charge?” Price on opens and niche CPM, adjust for placement, and present a clean band. Here’s the method.

Price a sponsorship How the numbers work

Price on opens, not subscribers

A sponsor is paying for attention, and attention is measured by the people who actually open the email — not your total list. Start with estimated opens = subscribers × open rate. A 10,000 subscriber list at a 40% open rate delivers ~4,000 opens per send. That 4,000 is what you price against.

Apply a niche CPM

CPM means cost per thousand opens. Multiply opens ÷ 1,000 by a CPM that reflects your niche. High-intent, commercially valuable niches (finance, B2B tech) support higher CPMs because a reader is worth more to an advertiser; broad lifestyle or general-interest lists sit lower. For the ranges we use, see the methodology page.

Base flat fee = (opens ÷ 1,000) × niche CPM. For 4,000 opens at a $35 CPM, that’s about $140 as a starting point for one placement.

Adjust for placement

Not every slot is equal. A sole/exclusive sponsor who owns the issue deserves a premium; a multi-sponsor send that shares attention should price lower per slot. A top, in-content placement is worth more than a footer classified. Bake this into a multiplier on the base fee.

Present a band, not a single number

Quote a low–high range around your target. It gives you room to negotiate, signals that rates depend on scope, and anchors the sponsor near your midpoint. Also state the implied CPM so a sophisticated buyer can compare you to other newsletters apples-to-apples.
